Green Home Score and Retrofit Recommendation Engine


1 Challenge
Many customers want an energy-efficient home but do not know where to start. Build an AI system that
analyses property characteristics, energy consumption and existing home features to:

1. calculate a Green Home Score;
2. identify homes with poor energy efficiency;
3. recommend suitable improvements, such as insulation, heat pumps, solar panels, double glazing
   and smart energy controls;
4. estimate potential energy savings and carbon reduction;
5. estimate the potential increase in property value.

The system should answer: How green is my home today, what should I improve, how much energy and
carbon could I save, and could this increase my property’s value?
Core rule. The score, savings, carbon and value figures come from documented models and calculations.
The LLM only explains them and answers what-if questions; it must not calculate the score or invent
estimates. For a what-if request, the relevant models and calculations must first be rerun for the proposed
scenario, and the LLM then explains their outputs.

3 Identify Poorly Performing Homes
Flag properties classified as Poor or Very poor and list their key issues (for example high energy use per
m2 , poor insulation, old heating system, single glazing, no renewable generation).
Rank properties by improvement potential: the estimated percentage reduction in annual purchased
energy achievable with the retrofits feasible for that property. For this metric, annual purchased energy
is the sum of grid electricity imported and gas consumed, in kWh/year. Calculate improvement potential
as 100 × (Ebaseline − Eafter )/Ebaseline , where both energy totals use the same accounting convention.
If baseline energy is zero or unavailable, report the metric as unavailable and document the handling.
Report electricity and gas changes separately, since a retrofit may reduce one while increasing the other.
Solar panels reduce grid imports, not the home’s underlying energy demand. A large house must not
be classed as inefficient solely because its total consumption is higher: consider energy intensity and
relevant property characteristics.


4 Retrofit Recommendations
Return a prioritised list of retrofits that are feasible for the property, not every retrofit available. Each
item gives its suitability (High, Medium or Low), estimated saving and reason. The order should reflect
how retrofits interact; for example, insulation before a heat pump. The impacts below are illustrative
and refer to the quantity named in each row; they are not directly comparable or additive. Any retrofit
catalogue used by the team takes precedence over these illustrative figures; teams must document its
source and assumptions.

    Retrofit                  Illustrative impact

    Roof or loft insulation   12–18% reduction in space-heating energy demand
    Heat pump                 15–25% reduction in purchased energy; electricity use may rise as gas use falls
    Solar panels              20–35% reduction in grid electricity imports
    Double glazing            8–12% reduction in space-heating energy demand
    Smart thermostat          5–10% reduction in heating energy use



5 Energy Savings
Estimate the saving for each recommended retrofit and for the package as a whole, in kWh/year, separately
for electricity and gas. Use a consistent baseline and estimate the post-retrofit consumption for each fuel.
Show an increase in consumption as a negative saving. For a package, account for interactions between
retrofits rather than simply adding their individual estimates.
Illustrative aggregate example. Baseline electricity use of 4,800 kWh/year plus gas use of 12,000 kWh/year
gives 16,800 kWh/year of purchased energy. If a retrofit is estimated to reduce this combined amount by
15%, the aggregate saving is 16,800 × 0.15 = 2,520 kWh/year. This aggregate example does not determine
the electricity and gas savings: the model must estimate and report the change for each fuel separately.
The full package may, for example, have a net estimated saving of 5,500 kWh/year after accounting for
interactions.
All savings are labelled as estimates, not guarantees.

6 Carbon Reduction
Convert energy savings to carbon using documented emissions factors for each fuel. Teams may use
suitable factors from a reliable source, with units and source recorded:
                                                 ∑︂
                                       ∆C =              ∆Ef × EFf
                                               f ∈{elec, gas}

where ∆Ef = Ef ,baseline − Ef ,after is the change in consumption of fuel f , in kWh/year, and EFf is its factor
in kg CO2 e per kWh. A positive ∆Ef is a saving; a negative value is an increase in consumption. A positive
∆C therefore represents an emissions reduction. Per-fuel calculation matters because a heat pump may
lower gas use while raising electricity use.


7 Property Value Impact
Estimate the potential change in property value as a range, not a single number.
Example. Current value £350,000; rating E to C; indicative uplift 2–5%; potential value £357,000 to £367,500.
Describe it as an estimated potential property-value impact based on historical and market relationships in
the dataset prepared by the team. Compare a consistent baseline with the proposed post-retrofit scenario
(for example, predicted values for the same property before and after the proposed improvements,
holding other property characteristics constant). Account for location, size, property type and market
conditions. If the available observational data and method cannot establish a causal retrofit effect,
describe the result as an indicative estimate rather than a guaranteed or proven causal increase.

Pipeline. Property, energy and sales data → Feature engineering → Green Home Score → Efficiency
detector → Retrofit engine → Savings, carbon and value models → LLM explanation → Customer dash-
board.
What-if flow. A what-if request updates the proposed retrofit scenario, reruns the relevant savings, carbon
and value calculations, and passes those computed results to the LLM for explanation. The LLM must not
invent scenario outputs.
Suggested methods. Score: explainable weighted scoring. Energy use: regression (XGBoost, LightGBM,
random forest, neural network). Retrofits: classification or recommendation model. Carbon: deterministic
calculation. Value: regression on property features, rating, retrofits and sales history. LLM: explains
the score and recommendations, and answers what-if questions using values computed by the other
components.

9 Dataset
Teams are expected to prepare the data needed for their solution. They may generate synthetic property,
energy, retrofit and sales data, use suitable publicly available datasets that may be lawfully used, or
combine both. Real personal or confidential customer data must not be used; public datasets must be
used in accordance with their licences and applicable restrictions. The tables below suggest entities
and fields. Teams may adapt them, provided their data supports the required outputs and functional
requirements. Teams must document data sources, schema, assumptions and limitations, and the
generation process and label-generation rules for synthetic data.
Teams must use property-level train, validation and test splits so that records for the same property do
not appear in multiple splits. Where multiple properties belong to one customer, or customer memory is
used, teams should split by customer to avoid leakage. Teams must also avoid using fields that would
not be available at prediction time. Monthly energy usage should be annualised consistently using the
available months; missing months or fields must be documented and handled explicitly rather than
treated as zero by default.

Teams must provide or construct the reference labels or estimates needed to evaluate the required outputs,
including improvement-potential rankings, retrofit feasibility/relevance, per-fuel savings, and property-
value impact. These may be derived from observed data, documented rules, suitable published sources, or
a simulator, as appropriate. Teams must document how the targets were obtained or generated. Synthetic
labels must reflect plausible relationships with the input features rather than being assigned arbitrarily.
If target values are generated by rules or a simulator, teams must disclose those rules and acknowledge
that agreement with synthetic targets alone does not establish real-world predictive accuracy.
Teams must use documented emissions factors for all carbon estimates, including units and sources.
Where appropriate factors are not included in their data, teams must source suitable factors and include
them with the submission. The data schema must distinguish the current property-value estimate used
as a baseline from historical sale prices, and identify fields unavailable at prediction time.
Because teams may use different datasets and label-generation methods, their numerical evaluation
results may not be directly comparable. Judges will consider data quality, the validity and documentation
of labels, held-out evaluation methodology, performance, and reproducibility.

10 Optional Extension: Customer Memory
Teams may give each customer a persistent memory, such as completed retrofits, stated constraints
or preferences, and past recommendations with the customer’s responses, and use it to adapt later
recommendations. The design is open. Scores and estimates must still come from the models and
calculations above. Teams that attempt this should show how recommendations change as memory
builds up.


11 Evaluation
Teams must evaluate their solutions using documented train, validation and test splits where applicable.
They must report their evaluation methodology, relevant metrics, baseline comparisons and limitations.
The quality of the data, label-generation process, validation methodology and reproducibility will be
considered alongside reported results.
Since teams may use different datasets and label-generation methods, numerical metrics from different
submissions are not directly comparable and will not be used as a standalone ranking criterion.

Scenario-based evaluation. During evaluation, organisers may test submissions using scenarios involving
missing or conflicting property data, infeasible or interacting retrofits, increased electricity use after a
heating-system change, and insufficient information to estimate savings or property-value impact.
LLM explanation evaluation. Organisers may evaluate explanations and what-if responses using held-out
scenarios, including missing data, conflicting signals and infeasible retrofits. Exact evaluation scenar-
ios need not be disclosed in advance. Targets are groundedness ≥90%, factual accuracy ≥90%, and
unsupported claims <5%.

Baseline and reproducibility. Teams must compare their approach with an appropriate simple baseline
